  Averaging arithmetic (background excluded): a level C held for t s inside a T-s window adds C t / T to the window mean.
  WHO 15-min: one 90-s red needs a 90-s mean of 873 ppm; a 10-s burst needs 7,856 ppm; a 1-s puff 78,561 ppm.
     staying through every red for the whole window (red fraction 0.45 of the cycle, exhaust cleared at green): 194 ppm mean during each red
     staying through every red for the whole window (red fraction 0.55 of the cycle, exhaust cleared at green): 159 ppm mean during each red
     staying through every red for the whole window (red fraction 0.65 of the cycle, exhaust cleared at green): 134 ppm mean during each red
  1-h (30 ppm): one 90-s red needs a 90-s mean of 1,200 ppm; a 10-s burst needs 10,800 ppm; a 1-s puff 108,000 ppm.
     staying through every red for the whole window (red fraction 0.45 of the cycle, exhaust cleared at green): 67 ppm mean during each red
     staying through every red for the whole window (red fraction 0.55 of the cycle, exhaust cleared at green): 55 ppm mean during each red
     staying through every red for the whole window (red fraction 0.65 of the cycle, exhaust cleared at green): 46 ppm mean during each red
  Highest-exposure kerb-side case in E (N=30, U=0.3, 0.5 m, 0.7 m): 13.8 ppm 90-s mean -> one red adds 1.38 ppm to a 15-min mean; standing through all reds at duty 0.55 gives 7.6 ppm 15-min mean.

G. BRIEF PEAKS - which references properly apply
  No CO health guideline has an averaging time shorter than 15 min except emergency/occupational ceilings:
  AEGL-2 10-min 420 ppm (disabling-effects emergency level), NIOSH ceiling 200 ppm (occupational, instantaneous).
  A 1-s or 10-s peak is compared ONLY (a) after converting to the 15-min/1-h window it contributes to, or (b) with these ceilings,
  clearly labelled as emergency/occupational. Peaks of tens to hundreds of ppm are physically possible in a plume core (D above);
  whether a nose sits there, and for how many seconds, is the question for the simulation.

H. ODOUR (perception, averaging time ~1 s)
  Whole petrol-engine exhaust dilution-to-threshold D/T_raw = 800-1,000 (Fiala 1967) to 5,200-6,800 (Swedish 1970) (epa-ap122-1973, abstracts).
  Odour units at the nose OU = D/T_raw / D  with D the dilution of exhaust at the nose; for the pack, D = raw-mix CO / CO increment.
  estimate dozen 90-s median   CO  0.29 ppm -> D    27,996 ->   0.03 to   0.24 OU
  estimate full box 90-s P95   CO  1.70 ppm -> D     4,776 ->   0.17 to   1.42 OU
  1 ppm                        CO  1.00 ppm -> D     8,119 ->   0.10 to   0.84 OU
  5 ppm (brief hit)            CO  5.00 ppm -> D     1,624 ->   0.49 to   4.19 OU
  20 ppm (core hit)            CO 20.00 ppm -> D       406 ->   1.97 to  16.75 OU
  HC-rich exhaust (failing tail ~5,800 ppm HC, two-strokes ~5,400 ppm, vs ~400-500 ppm ordinary; MOENV 2025 / Taipei Times):
  if odorants scale with HC, such a scooter's D/T_raw is ~10-14x higher (ASSUMPTION), i.e. ~10,000-90,000.
  Reading: at the estimate's 90-s means the pack is at or below the detection threshold on average; seconds-long plume
  hits of 1-20 ppm CO carry 0.1-50 OU (detectable to strong), and an HC-rich neighbour adds 10x. A horrible smell needs
  only seconds-scale hits; it does not imply CO near 87 ppm over 15 min. CO itself is odourless.
